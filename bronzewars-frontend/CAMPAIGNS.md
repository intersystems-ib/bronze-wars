# Campaigns

The application starts on the campaign home screen with two choices: Start new campaign and Load ongoing campaign. New campaigns go through nation selection (Egypt, Hatti and Nubia) and always start from zero under a fresh game ID. Loading opens a registry showing each game's ID, player nation and current year/season, ordered by latest update. Multiple games of the same nation remain independent. Each campaign uses `src/assets/map/egypt.png`. Sea Peoples are an invading AI faction, unavailable in nation selection; after founding a capital they can recruit normally. Their existing tactical battle roster remains available through the main menu.

## Initial rules

- A new campaign starts in 1300 BC, during Inundation, with 40 grain for Nubia or 100 grain for the other nations, and no recruited armies.
- Each year has three turns: Inundation, Emergence and Harvest. After Harvest, the next year starts (1299 BC, 1298 BC, etc.). Season names follow the [Egyptian calendar](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendario_egipcio).
- Recruitment reuses the battle army builder, national profiles and grain costs returned by the existing unit catalogue. The campaign's remaining reserve is the recruitment budget; there is no separate 100-grain limit per army. The nation is fixed for the campaign and cannot be changed in the recruitment modal; the rules reject recruitment with a different nation.
- Each army stores its composition and nation and appears with that nation's light infantry sprite. New armies deploy in a free land hex of their current capital region, initially Memphis, Karkemish or Faras. The city hex is preferred, followed by nearby hexes in stable breadth-first order. Recruitment is available only while inspecting the player's own capital. An occupied city hex does not block recruitment while free hexes remain in its region. Only a full current capital region or having no controlled city blocks deployment without spending grain. Occupied hexes, neutral land, water and other city regions are excluded. Existing armies keep their saved positions.
- Army sidebar cards show total surviving troop strength and morale beside the national light infantry icon. Selecting an army replaces the list with only that army's card and composition; Back to your armies clears selection and restores the list. The list offers Go to the capital to inspect the recruitment location; there is no global recruitment button. A formation is distinct from its troops: one light infantry unit starts with 60 troops, while Hittite chariots start with 15 (Egyptian chariots have 20). Recruitment reads the national catalogue's `initialStrength` and stores a separate current-strength entry for every formation. Morale starts at 100 and follows seasonal attrition and recovery, as detailed below. Selecting an army shows its troop types as national sprites; counters underneath show the number of formations with positive strength. Tooltips show each type's current/maximum troop strength. Army names remain available in accessible labels and tooltips.
- Movement is the unit-count-weighted mean of the composition's speeds. Only complete hexes can be crossed (a mean of 1.6 allows one hex per turn). Entering desert or mountains exhausts the remaining allowance. An army starting on either difficult terrain can move to just one neighboring hex, exhausting the turn even when leaving it. Paths cannot cross an intermediate difficult hex. Movement cannot be banked between seasons.
- Reachable destinations use breadth-first pathfinding and account for occupied hexes, difficult terrain and approximate coastlines. Rivers are traversable; sea hexes are not. Terrain regions are authored approximations of the supplied illustration in `src/config/campaignTerrain.js`: Nile valley/delta, desert, mountains and water. Each terrain type includes its movement restriction and attrition rate. Naval movement remains unavailable. Campaign encounters use the resolution rules below.
- At the end of each season, deployed troops consume grain in proportion to surviving strength (sum of each type’s current troops / maximum force per formation × grainCost × 0.1). Consumption can be fractional, but remaining reserves round up to a whole number after settlement and when loading old saves. Recruitment uses the same rounded budget: 27.8 becomes 28, can pay for a 28-grain army and leaves zero, while 29 remains unaffordable. Consumption is based on actual force before that season's losses or recovery: full strength costs 10% of recruitment cost, half strength costs 5%. National consumption rounds once to two decimals after adding all armies, so splitting or merging troops does not change the bill. The same calculation feeds AI recruitment capacity and supply settlement.
- Grain is shared by all armies. Situation is evaluated at the beginning of each season's settlement: empty reserves mean famine (5% supply casualties); positive reserves with production greater than upkeep mean abundance (recover 5% of missing troops); otherwise the situation is scarcity (no supply casualties or recovery, including equal production/consumption).
- Armies ending a turn in desert or mountains outside their own territory suffer 3% terrain casualties, even if they did not move. Own territory prevents terrain casualties; neutral and enemy territory do not. Current city ownership determines protection on city hexes. Terrain inspection and map labels show the effective rate for the player's nation. Difficult movement restrictions still apply in own territory, as do famine and assault losses. Terrain losses apply first, followed by supply losses on surviving troops when applicable. Each percentage rounds up once on the army's combined current troop strength; losses are allocated proportionally among individual formations with largest remainders. Casualties reduce force, not formation count. A formation ceases contributing to movement at zero force and its upkeep decreases proportionally with casualties; an army is removed when all its formations have zero force. Movement remains the mean of active formations' speeds, and upkeep scales each type’s recruitment cost by its remaining force.
- The saved state retains turn settlement details internally. The campaign screen omits the last-turn balance section; terrain inspection still shows terrain and attrition.
- City production belonging to the selected nation is credited only at the end of Harvest, once per year. Inundation and Emergence credit no grain. Each season still subtracts upkeep and rounds the nonnegative remainder up. The starting reserve determines famine even if the annual harvest covers the bill that season. Other nations and unowned hexes do not contribute to the selected nation's reserve. Movement resets after settlement. The saved turn number determines the harvest date, preventing duplicate annual income after reloading.
- Recovery rounds up once per army based on 5% of its missing troop strength and distributes recovered troops proportionally among individual formations. Every formation is capped at its own original `initialStrength`. Zero-strength formations can recover while the army survives; completely destroyed armies do not reappear.
- Terrain attrition outside own territory remains independent of supply situation: it applies before supply casualties or recovery. Scarcity prevents supply penalties, not terrain casualties.
- Cities are fixed settlements defined in `src/config/campaignCities.js`, on distinct land hexes inside their nation's territory. Each nation has exactly one capital. Egypt has six cities producing 31 grain annually; Hatti has six producing 30; Nubia has three producing 20. Nubian cities occupy west-bank land hexes beside the Nile, away from the painted water channel. Each city contributes a fixed 2–8 grain per Harvest to the shared national reserve. There is no additional income from surrounding terrain or water. Existing saves immediately use the new city production without resetting reserves, armies or calendar.
- Cities display `src/assets/map/city.png`, their localized name and a star with a national-color ring for capitals. Map labels do not show grain amounts. Selecting a city shows its nation, capital/city designation, underlying terrain and annual yield. Cities preserve the movement and attrition rules of their underlying terrain and can be occupied by armies. Capitals grant no additional economic or combat effects in this phase.
- From north to south, Egyptian cities are Naucratis, Memphis (capital), Heracleopolis, Thinis, Thebes and Elephantine; Nubian cities are Faras (capital), Kerma and Napata. Hatti's capital is Karkemish. Its coast has Byblos, Sidon and Gaza from north to south. Gaza moves from visible hex 23,6 to 16,7; Megiddo moves from 18,8 to 17,8, with Damascus to its north. Names are localized in Spanish and English.
- Coast is traversable land with zero terrain attrition. Coastline classification samples the centre, corners and edge midpoints: a mixed land/sea hex is Coast even if its centre is in the water. Land adjacent to open sea also has coastal access. Open sea remains impassable to armies.
- Clicking a non-movement destination switches the left sidebar from armies to terrain details and clears army selection. Clicking an army or using Back to your armies restores the army view; valid movement clicks retain it. Terrain details show territory, river where applicable, attrition, production and movement. Grain uses Unicode U+131E3.
- Egypt and Nubia follow the Nile valley/delta plus two neighboring land hexes on either side, using hex-grid distance. Egypt includes visible row 34; Nubia starts at row 35. Remote deserts remain neutral. Hatti covers the Jordan, Euphrates and Levant coast; Sinai is explicitly neutral. Borders follow hex edges: Egyptian yellow, Hittite blue and Nubian purple. Shared boundaries render both nations' colors on their own side with a 4px stroke. Borders are currently visual and do not restrict army movement.

## Raiding and assaulting cities

The selected-army sidebar also offers Disband army, followed by an inline confirmation or cancellation. Confirming removes that friendly army immediately, including any assault order, without refunding recruitment grain or advancing the season. Its hex becomes free, vision and upkeep are recalculated, and the removal is saved with the campaign. City ownership and annual raid history remain unchanged. Enemy armies cannot be disbanded by the player.

An army occupying a city controlled by another nation can choose Raid or Assault from its selected-army sidebar. Starting either action exhausts its remaining seasonal movement. Simply entering an enemy city neither captures it nor grants grain. Friendly cities and non-city hexes cannot be targeted.

- Raiding immediately awards the city's effective grain yield (halved while occupied) to the raider once for the current year. The city produces no grain for any nation at that year's Harvest, even if the army leaves, is destroyed, or the city changes hands. A saved per-city year marker prevents repeated looting by the same or another army during that year. In the following year normal production resumes and the city can be raided again. Raiding does not change ownership. A non-player nation's loot is retained in its separate `foreignGrain` reserve; it is not added to the player's reserve. Sea Peoples may raid, assault and capture cities and their regions.
- Assault duration is fixed on commencement at `ceil(3000 / initial surviving troops)`, with a minimum of one turn: 1,000 troops require 3 turns and 500 require 6. It counts individual troop strength, not formation count. Each end of season applies 5% assault casualties to surviving troops, rounded up once per army after terrain and supply casualties. Abundance recovery is suspended for assaulting armies. Falling troop strength does not extend the original deadline.
- Leaving or explicitly interrupting an assault discards its progress. Interruption never refunds movement already spent. A destroyed army does not capture the city. On the final assault turn, a surviving army takes permanent control of the city, its entire predefined region and its future production. A previously raided city remains unproductive until the year ends. An unraided city captured on Harvest contributes to its new owner's harvest immediately.
- All 263 national land hexes belong to exactly one of the 15 city regions. Regions are fixed before play using simultaneous breadth-first growth from the cities within their original national land; deterministic ties and land adjacency guarantee every region connects to its city without detached hexes. Water, Sinai and other neutral land remain outside city regions. Terrain inspection identifies the region's city. Capturing or recapturing a city transfers every hex in its region atomically, including borders, vision and terrain attrition protection. No neighboring region changes ownership. Regions remain fixed after conquest; this guarantees continuity within each region, not that every nation stays connected after losing a central region.
- City ownership, raid markers and ongoing assault progress are saved per game. Regions derive their current owner from the saved city owner, so older city-only conquests automatically extend to their full regions without resetting the game. Static map data and other saved games are unchanged. A nation recruits while inspecting its current capital and deploys in a free land hex of its region. If the original capital is lost, a controlled city becomes the provisional capital; recovering the original restores it immediately.
- The same action functions accept an acting nation for future campaign AI orders. Once an AI army has an assault order, that assault resolves with the same timing and casualties at each world turn. Player reports never expose hidden enemy casualty counts, but do report cities lost by the player. Autonomous movement, assault and raid orders remain future work; AI recruitment is implemented below.

## Campaign AI recruitment

Regional deployment requires a current browser bundle. Requests must include `deploymentArea: capital_region` and a nonnegative integer `freeDeploymentHexes`, consistent with `capitalAvailable`; old city-only requests are rejected with `CAMPAIGN_CLIENT_OUTDATED` rather than reported to Qwen as a full region. Reloading the current client reconsiders unversioned LLM wait decisions for the current turn once, while preserving recruitment orders, existing armies, reserves and explicit player skips. New decisions carry `deploymentRuleVersion: 3`; prior non-skipped wait decisions are reconsidered once under the provisional-capital rule, while completed recruitments remain final for that turn. The testing view displays the acting rival's actual free capital-region hex count inside its decision modal.

At the start of every season, including the initial turn, local Qwen decides once for each of the two rival territorial nations whether to recruit one army or wait. A native modal dialog blocks interaction while decisions are being made. It shows the currently acting nation's emblem and name, the recruitment phase and progress through pending rivals. It updates when the next nation begins and closes automatically after all requests complete. Escape does not dismiss an active decision. Focus and body scrolling are restored on close; failures return to the existing Retry/Skip controls. Completed decisions are saved per nation and turn; loading a game never repeats a completed decision or creates duplicate armies. Older saves initialize Nubia with 40 grain and other rivals with 100 unless that nation already has a saved reserve (for example from raids). Sea Peoples join the AI phases after their first invasion, with recruitment disabled until they found a capital.

When the current capital region is full or no controlled city remains, the tool reports zero deployable formations of every type and no army options. capitalAvailable refers to available deployment space in the whole capital region; freeDeploymentHexes reports its free hex count. The model reads this real tool result and chooses a wait decision; no synthetic successful decision is substituted.

The frontend sends only the acting nation's economic snapshot to `POST /api/bronzewars/campaign/recruitment`, routed through Campaign Service and LLM Operation. The server invokes the audited `GetRecruitmentCapacity` tool through the agent's ToolManager before calling the model, validates that consultation succeeded and includes its actual result in the decision prompt. This prerequisite is guaranteed by orchestration rather than depending on the LLM choosing to call a tool. A failed or expired tool context still prevents a decision. The LLM chooses recruit or wait from the supplied data; its response does not need an additional tool call. The temporary, random context is removed after success or failure. The snapshot is calculated from the campaign rules: reserves, city production per year, upkeep per turn and year, seasons until Harvest, current troops/armies, capital availability and national unit costs/forces. Bounded knapsack computes the maximum troop strength affordable now and the maximum maintainable with annual production after existing yearly upkeep. These maxima respect the 99-formations-per-type limit; different type maxima are alternatives, not additive allowances. The projected sustainable capacity remains advisory only after the current annual production covers existing annual upkeep and reserves can fund at least four divisions. A proposed army may exceed future sustainable capacity, but a current production deficit prevents the recruitment phase entirely.

Both server and frontend reject unknown unit codes, fractional or invalid quantities, unaffordable compositions and recruitment in a full current capital region or without any controlled city. Recruitments spend only that rival's reserve and deploy within its current capital region with national unit profiles and a globally unique army ID. Rivals pay upkeep every turn, harvest only at year end, and suffer terrain/famine casualties or recover by the same strength rules. For AI economic planning and supply, abundance means annual production exceeds three turns of upkeep. Their balances and casualty reports remain separate from the player's. No deterministic army-creation fallback overrides an eligible model decision.

The capacity tool also offers small, balanced and large army options with exact national compositions, troop counts, costs, resulting upkeep and remaining reserves. Qwen can choose one by `optionId`, propose a custom composition or wait; choosing an option never bypasses composition/budget validation. A malformed or invalid proposal gets one correction request to the same model before an error is returned. Agent initialization explicitly loads its instructions and toolset before session creation; session options use the installed AI-Core API (`max_iterations`, `max_tokens`, `timeout_ms`) and model selection comes from the agent's model parameter.

If the model is unavailable or returns an invalid order, the turn pauses with Retry and an explicit Skip decision option. Skipping records a wait decision for that nation and turn. The testing checkbox Disable fog of war reveals the map and all enemy formation counts and shows the acting nation's free deployment count in the decision modal. There is no persistent decisions panel or thinking notice above the map after the phase ends. It is a local display option, off when reopening a game, and never supplies extra information to the AI or changes army control. Recruitment is followed by strategic movement decisions for that nation.

## Fog of war

Each nation sees its entire territory clearly, plus its armies' hexes and the immediate ring around them. Other hexes are shaded, including their cities and borders. Vision uses geometric hex distance across land and water and updates immediately after movement; previously visited foreign hexes are shaded again when no army provides vision.

Army markers display the number of surviving formations, rather than their army ID or troop strength. All enemy armies inside the observing nation's current territory are detected with their active formation count, even without any friendly army nearby. This follows whole-region ownership after conquest. Outside national territory, enemies are detected only within two hexes of a friendly army: at exactly two hexes the marker displays `?`; adjacent enemies show their active formation count. More distant foreign enemies have no marker, tooltip or accessible army label. Enemy contacts cannot be selected or moved as friendly armies and never expose their names, morale or composition. Only friendly armies contribute to the player's upkeep and detailed casualty reports. Enemy assault orders resolve each world turn without revealing hidden enemy casualty counts.

`src/config/campaignVisibility.js` exports `campaignView(campaign, nation)` as the shared observation function for human interfaces and future campaign AI controllers. It returns clear hex IDs, friendly armies and limited enemy contacts, without hidden enemy data. The optional `{ reveal: true }` third argument is used only by the testing checkbox. Campaign strategy uses this same observation; the debug checkbox never changes its input.

## Persistence and verification

Each game is stored in the current browser's local storage under `bronzewars.campaign.save.<id>`, independently of saved tactical battles in IRIS. A single atomic record contains save format version, ID, creation/update timestamps and the full campaign state (nation, season, reserves, armies, strengths, morale, positions, movement and last-turn report). The ID is shown in the campaign header. Every state change saves automatically to that game's record. Returning home or refreshing the browser preserves all records; loading resumes the same ID and state. Starting a new game never overwrites an earlier campaign. This phase does not synchronize campaigns between browsers or devices.

Opening the registry imports valid old per-nation saves once into stable `legacy-<nation>` game IDs. The original `bronzewars.campaign.<nation>.v1` entries are kept as backups, and campaigns without an explicit nation continue as Egyptian. Importing again never resets an already imported game. Malformed records and strength-conversion backups are excluded from the registry; loading an invalid or missing record reports an error instead of silently starting a new game. Failed storage writes retain the previous saved state.

Hatti uses the `hatti` nation ID and `HITTITE` faction, with `_hatti` artwork filenames. Legacy campaign identities and storage keys are converted when loading, preserving the original storage entry as a backup and preferring an existing Hatti save. Saved tactical army design IDs also resolve to Hatti. The IRIS seed migrates existing armies and unit profiles in a transaction before seeding the Hittite catalogue, preserving battle unit strengths and deployment positions. Legacy identifiers remain only in compatibility code.

The current save format is version 2 (the storage key is kept for compatibility). Version 1 campaigns upgrade when the national unit catalogue is available: surviving formations get their actual national force, missing formations recorded by the old roster become zero-force recovery slots, and date, grain, morale, positions and names are preserved. A copy is kept under the `.before-strength-v2` suffix before replacement. Old turn reports are discarded because their casualty counts referred to whole formations, not troops. Turn advancement/recruitment wait for this conversion; no guessed force values are used.

The campaign rules live in `src/config/campaignRules.js`. Run `npm test` for recruitment, shared reserves, movement, blocked hexes, seasons and persistence checks. Run `npm run build` to validate the production frontend.


## Strategic movement and campaign encounters

After recruitment, the audited GetCampaignSituation and GetArmyReinforcementAdvice tools supply Qwen with batches of up to four own armies, their force, morale, movement, previous mission, reserves, production, all national invaders, nearby foreign contacts and legal strategic options. Defence takes priority over foreign offensives: at least one reachable defensive order is required while an invader is detected. Otherwise Qwen chooses recovery, raiding, conquest, continuing a siege or holding position. Recovery uses the existing seasonal supply rules, never instant reinforcements. Recruitment also offers fast raiding and conquest compositions, with normal national prices and profiles.

The server validates option identifiers, one order per army and defence priority. The client independently validates and executes each order with ordinary land adjacency, occupied hexes and difficult-terrain movement. Paths use known contacts; unseen blockers stop execution without exposing their data to the model. City locations come from known cartography; ownership and raid availability outside clear vision are checked when reached. Raids and sieges use the existing yearly raid ledger and multi-season assault rules. Decisions and completed army IDs are saved per nation and turn, so interrupted phases resume remaining orders. The modal shows recruitment, movement or combat with the acting nation's emblem. Model failures allow retry or explicit skip.

Adjacent hostile armies can engage when the attacker has movement left. Engagement spends its remaining movement and saves a unique pending encounter; world turns and campaign orders pause until it resolves. A human army can inspect an adjacent contact and choose Attack army.

When both participants are AI, no tactical engine is invoked. Force is the sum of surviving troops times the mean of national attack, defence and damage, multiplied by (0.5 + morale / 200); the defender receives a 10% advantage. The stronger side wins (defender on ties). Winner casualties are ceil(troops * min(35%, 10% + 25% * opposing power / own power)), retaining at least one survivor. Loser casualties are ceil(troops * min(85%, 40% + 20% * opposing power / own power)). Losses are distributed across formations by the shared troop rules. The loser loses half its morale, abandons sieges and retreats through unoccupied land up to three hexes to a free hex at least two hexes from the winner, heading towards the nearest reachable friendly city when possible, otherwise preferring its own territory. If escape is impossible, its remaining troops are lost. Both armies exhaust movement. The campaign retains the latest 50 casualty/retreat reports; the attacker occupies the defender's hex only on victory.

Human encounters create an idempotent IRIS tactical battle keyed by game and encounter IDs. Both rosters use exact arriving strengths, national profiles and morale, with stable formation slots; destroyed formations stay absent. There is no separate 100-grain limit or newly generated opposing army. The AI is predeployed, the player deploys normally and the existing tactical movement/combat interface is used. Displayed force and defeat percentages use the national theoretical maximum; the outcome summary uses arriving strengths so prior campaign wounds are not counted as new tactical deaths. Exiting an unfinished battle leaves a resumable encounter; finishing and returning to campaign applies surviving winner formations and morale, restores escaped defeated formations as a retreating army, and resumes pending AI orders. Dead troops and prisoners do not return. The pending battle ID survives reloads and retries never create another battle for the same encounter.

Verification includes campaignMovement.test.js and BronzeWars.Tests.CampaignStrategyTest for fog-limited strategic contexts, mandatory defence, unique legal orders, saved progress, raids/sieges, recovery, movement, automatic casualties/retreat and tactical roster conservation through battle start and surrender.


The saved campaign registry offers Delete beside each Load action. A native confirmation dialog identifies the game and nation, warns that removal is permanent and defaults focus to Cancel. Confirmation removes that campaign record and its strength-conversion backup without changing other campaigns or tactical battle records. Imported legacy games also remove their old per-nation source entries. A small deletion marker prevents legacy reimport and rejects stale autosaves from other open tabs, so deleted games cannot reappear. Storage errors are reported in the registry. Tests use isolated in-memory storage for deletion, preservation of other games and legacy/stale-save protection.


Strategic response parsing tolerates one narrowly identified Qwen formatting error: when every string, array and nested object is complete and only the final root-object brace is absent, it appends that closing brace and then runs the full JSON and military-order validation. It never infers fields, option IDs, unfinished text or missing array elements. Malformed order shapes receive explicit errors; failed correction diagnostics include a bounded excerpt of the actual response. Regression tests cover the reported defence response, escaped delimiters, fenced JSON and invalid or incomplete orders.


## Occupied territory and reconquest

Occupation is derived from each fixed city region's original nation and current city owner, with no new save format. All hexes of a region are occupied while those nations differ; neutral land is never occupied. Taking an occupied region from another occupier retains its original owner. Reclaiming the city clears occupation across its entire region. The map marks occupied hexes with diagonal hatching and localized labels; terrain details show Occupied and Original owner alongside the current controller.

Recruitment capacity and strategic context include the acting nation's occupied original regions (city, known occupier, capital, annual production and region size). This political knowledge remains available under fog but never reveals remote garrisons. Defence against detected invaders remains first priority. With no such invaders, RECLAIM takes priority over new foreign conquests and raids, with capital targets offered first. Qwen must select at least one reachable RECLAIM option for the current batch when that priority applies; other armies may recover or hold. Both client and server enforce it. Without a route, no impossible order is required.

RECLAIM uses ordinary movement and battles and starts or continues the existing city assault on arrival. Victory over a garrison preserves that mission and starts the siege, in automatic AI combat and human tactical results. Only completing the assault restores regional ownership; merely entering or raiding the city does not. Reconquest missions and per-turn progress persist in existing saves. Existing campaign saves immediately derive occupation from their saved cityOwners, and new strategic priorities apply when that nation's next decision phase runs.


## Recruitment limits and fast economic checks

Before an AI recruitment request, compare annual city production with existing upkeep across all three seasons. If production is strictly lower, record a RULES wait decision with PRODUCTION_DEFICIT, without a model request or recruitment modal. If rounded spendable reserves cannot fund four copies of the cheapest available national division, record INSUFFICIENT_GRAIN the same way. Equality permits recruitment. These rules apply to both client orchestration and the server before agent/tool initialization; eligible decisions still require the normal audited tool consultation. Skipping recruitment does not skip strategic movement, defence, recovery or reconquest. Decisions remain saved once per nation and turn.

Every newly recruited campaign army and newly created standalone tactical army requires at least four divisions in total, counting quantities across types; four divisions of a single type are valid. Both campaign and tactical creation screens disable submission and show the localized minimum message below that count. Campaign creation also requires enough grain for the cheapest four divisions before opening recruitment. All AI preset compositions start with four affordable divisions and are filtered out if their minimum is unaffordable. Custom model proposals are validated by client and server. Existing or depleted armies may remain below four and campaign tactical encounters retain their actual surviving rosters; the creation rule never deletes armies or supplies free divisions.


## Army merging, morale and occupation economy

Moving onto a reachable friendly army opens a native confirmation dialog for the human player, with the resulting troop force, formation count and morale. Cancelling preserves both armies. Confirming retains the destination army's identity and hex, removes the source, and exhausts the combined army's movement until the next season. Friendly occupied hexes are valid merge destinations but cannot be crossed. AI MERGE orders use the same rules without confirmation, can approach allies across several seasons, and mark both participants processed when the merge completes.

Formations of the same type combine only when their total force fits within that type's maximum: 30 + 50 out of 100 becomes 80; 30 + 80 remains two formations. Other types remain distinct. Troops are conserved, destroyed slots are discarded, and merged formations determine upkeep and movement normally. Morale is weighted by each army's surviving troops and rounded to an integer. Existing merged armies may exceed recruitment's 99-formations-per-type limit, including in saved games and tactical campaign encounters; new recruitment retains its existing limits.

Before each model strategy request, GetArmyReinforcementAdvice compares each moving army's active formation count with detected enemies within two hexes. It recommends merging when a known enemy count is greater and a friendly merge candidate is reachable. Unknown contacts remain unknown, and troop force is not substituted for formation count. Both strategic tools execute through the audited ToolManager before the same model call. Recommended MERGE orders may prepare defence or reconquest instead of immediately attacking a superior enemy; other priority checks remain enforced.

Terrain attrition, famine and city assault also reduce current morale by the same sequential percentages as troop force, rounding each loss up and clamping at zero. Abundance recovers 5% of missing morale, rounded up and capped at 100, under the same conditions as troop recovery. Reports show morale lost and recovered.

An occupied city produces exactly half its normal yield for its current controller until liberated, including fractional yields. Raid suppression still sets production to zero for that year. Fractions are retained when summing annual income; only the resulting national grain balance rounds up. Occupying armies receive no terrain attrition and no troop or morale recovery in occupied regions. Famine and assault losses still apply. Recovery options direct wounded occupying armies toward their own original territory. Existing saves derive these effects from their city ownership without migration.


## AI battle result announcements

Each newly resolved AI-versus-AI encounter pauses the acting nation's decision modal on a readable battle result. The localized battle name combines the terrain with the city of that hex's fixed region; neutral land instead names the nearest settlement without claiming a region. The result shows the victorious nation, both sides' troop casualties, and whether the defeated army retreated or was destroyed. Continue acknowledges the result and resumes the remaining orders. There is no timed dismissal.

Results store their geographic context and an explicit presented flag in the existing campaign combat history. Reloading before acknowledgment restores the announcement without rerunning combat or applying casualties again. Older reports without this flag remain historical and do not generate a backlog. Human tactical encounters retain their existing flow.


Strategic option identifiers are copied from the canonical options included in the context supplied both initially and on correction. DEFEND and MERGE identify the target army, while targetHexId is its destination. If Qwen substitutes a coordinate destination in an otherwise identical army/intent identifier, the server canonicalizes it only when exactly one existing legal option matches that army, intent and hex. Exact IDs take precedence. Unknown or ambiguous coordinates, changed intents and other armies remain invalid. Canonical IDs then pass all normal uniqueness, batch and priority checks before the client receives them; no replacement strategy is invented.


Campaign strategy requests use a compact instruction set and omit army-local mergeCandidates already supplied by the audited reinforcement advice, retaining every enemy contact, own combat statistic and legal option. The initial reply allows 768 output tokens; the single correction allows 1,024 and starts a fresh agent session containing the complete same strategic context and a bounded validation error, never the failed response/history. A reply cut inside a string or order remains invalid: missing military decisions are never reconstructed. Both sessions allow 60 seconds per request and require the complete JSON with a one-sentence reason. The Qwen server's default context is now 8,192 tokens (QWEN_CONTEXT_SIZE can override it), increased from 4,096 after logs showed prompts and corrections exhausting the context at 4,095 tokens.


## Tactical withdrawal completion

Inactive formations that still have troops are withdrawn survivors, not remaining combatants. Surrender metrics retain their initial force in the baseline but exclude their troops and morale from active fighting strength. When the last formation leaves, the battle finishes immediately with WITHDRAWAL if the defeated army has fled survivors, or ANNIHILATION when none survive. The existing result summary records surviving fugitives as fled without adding deaths or prisoners. Partial withdrawals do not end a battle while combatants remain.

Loading an in-progress battle also checks for an army with no active positive-strength formations and reconciles previously stuck battles transactionally. Deployment and already-finished battles remain unchanged; repeated reads do not duplicate the finish event. Turn preparation performs the same check and returns the finished result before requesting another AI movement decision. Regression coverage in BronzeWars.Tests.BattleWithdrawalTest includes same-turn final withdrawal, reload recovery, both sides' victory, annihilation, partial withdrawals and outcome persistence.


## Theoretical force percentages

Unit inspectors, encounter details, army strength/morale bars, automatic defeat metrics and tactical AI context use each national unit profile's full initialStrength. Arriving campaign strength never replaces that maximum: 32 of a theoretical 60 troops shows 53%, while national maxima such as 15 or 20 chariots are retained. Army percentages divide combined surviving active force and troop-weighted morale by the sum of theoretical maxima for the formations taking part. Destroyed or withdrawn formations remain in that capacity but do not contribute active force or morale. Missing formations destroyed before entering the battle are not recreated.

CampaignInitialStrength remains the arrival baseline only for the final battle outcome: initialForces and casualties describe this encounter, preserving prior campaign wounds without reporting them again as new tactical casualties. Frontend battleMetrics tests and CampaignStrategyTest cover the theoretical denominator, AI assessment and movement, and unchanged battle-loss accounting.


## Automatic provisional capitals

campaignCapital derives the current capital from city ownership. While its original capital is controlled, that city always retains or resumes the role. Otherwise the controlled city with the greatest regular production becomes the provisional capital. Occupied cities count at half yield; temporary raids do not move capitals. Ties use city number and then ID for deterministic saves. Further conquests or losses recalculate the eligible cities; a nation without any controlled city has no capital and cannot recruit. The Sea Peoples founding capital is their first conquered city; if lost, a provisional capital uses the same production rule and the founding capital resumes its role upon recapture.

Capital stars, national-color rings, settlement details, the Go to capital shortcut, army creation and region deployment all follow the same current capital. AI recruitment capacity supplies capitalCityId and capitalHexId and strategic context identifies the current capital, while lost-region priority still targets the original capital for reconquest. Recruitment decisions use deploymentRuleVersion 3 to reconsider old wait decisions once without repeating completed recruitments or explicit skips. Capitals derive directly from saved cityOwners, so existing campaigns adopt this rule without changing ownership, calendar, resources or army locations. campaignCapitals.test.js covers all three nations, successive losses, raids, ties, occupied foreign cities, no remaining cities, human and AI recruitment, save/load and restoration after a completed assault.


## Surviving armies in retreat

Returning from a finished tactical campaign battle reconstructs the defeated army's escaped formations from their campaignSlot, currentStrength, morale and inactive status. It preserves army identity, national profiles and exact escaped force; dead and surrendered active formations (prisoners) do not become fugitives. Both participants exhaust their movement and are marked processed for that nation's current AI phase, preventing queued offensive orders from replacing the retreat. Finished encounters remain idempotent through pendingEncounter clearing.

Tactical and automatic AI battles share retreatAfterBattle. Immediate disengagement moves through up to three free land hexes towards the nearest reachable friendly city, avoiding occupied hexes and cities held by enemy garrisons. If a friendly garrison occupies the destination city, a free adjacent hex in its region provides the rally point. With no friendly city, survivors use a legal fallback escape hex; a fully blocked army cannot escape. No new force or morale is granted during retreat.

A saved RETREAT mission advances automatically once each season before AI decisions, for human and AI armies alike. Later movement follows ordinary speed, terrain costs and blockers. Reaching the rally point changes the mission to RECOVER; a captured target is replaced by another reachable friendly city. Retreating armies remain deployed and retain ordinary upkeep, supply, terrain attrition and recovery rules. They are excluded from LLM movement batches until the retreat ends. Reloading does not give extra movement.

Visible army markers show a retreat arrow and tooltips identify the retreat status. Friendly army details name its rally city; enemy contacts reveal only the observable retreat flag, never the destination or hidden composition. Fog rules still hide distant armies. Automatic combat result modals name the retreat city. campaignRetreat.test.js covers exact tactical survivors and morale, prisoner exclusion, human and AI defeats, seasonal movement and persistence, changed targets, friendly garrisons, missing cities and blocked escape routes.


## Compact campaign modifiers

The sidebar replaces situation explanations with Modifiers: +5% troops, +5% morale (localized in Spanish and English). Positive effects are green, negative effects red, and zero effects neutral. Without an army selected, this summarizes supply: abundance +5%, famine -5%, scarcity 0%. Selecting a friendly army also accounts for its effective terrain attrition and assault losses, and suppresses recovery for occupying garrisons and besieging armies. Simultaneous effects remain separate rather than adding percentages based on surviving troops and missing troops; short source tooltips identify each effect.

The Last turn balance section and repeated saved-locally, annual-production and fog explanations are removed from the campaign screen. No settlement rules, historical save data or battle-result modals are changed. campaignModifiers.test.js verifies supply states, difficult terrain, own-territory protection, occupied garrisons and assaults.


Army names
----------
New human and AI armies draw a random name from the persistent national repertoires in `src/config/campaignArmyNames.js`. Egypt, Hatti and Nubia each combine leaders and deities from their civilization's wider history; these honorific names are not restricted to rulers alive in the campaign year. The recruitment form starts with an editable suggestion. Blank names use an automatic name. Names already deployed by that nation are skipped in either supported language; exhausted repertoires use numbered suffixes. The chosen string is stored in the existing army name field, so saves, battles and retreats retain it without rerolling or changing old names. Spanish and English recruitment use localized prefixes and personal names.

Historical naming references: [Egyptian deities (UCL)](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/religion/deitiescreation.html), [Hittite rulers (The Met)](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/the-hittites), [Amun and Taharqa (British Museum)](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA1779), [Amanirenas (British Museum)](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA1650).


Sea Peoples invasions
---------------------
`campaignSeaPeoples.js` schedules the first invasion 2–10 years after a new or migrated campaign starts using this feature; subsequent waves are 2–10 years apart (6–30 seasons). The chosen schedule, processed season, wave number, ship positions, destinations and embarked rosters persist in each campaign save. Reload/retry cannot repeat movement or launch another wave in the same season.

Each wave launches 1–3 ships, with independent landing destinations along the Mediterranean coasts of Egypt, Sinai and the Levant. Only the connected Mediterranean water component is navigable; Red Sea landings are excluded. Ships originate 3–8 sea hexes from their destination and move two hexes each season. The supplied `units/ship_seapeople.png` renders each ship with a turquoise national outline. Fog reveals ships only within two hexes of friendly armies (or with the reveal checkbox); observations contain neither cargo nor destination.

Every ship carries a full-strength army of at least four divisions, randomly assembled from the national infantry, spearmen and archer profiles for a maximum recruitment value of 50 grain. Ships reserve unique army IDs. Occupied beaches are replaced by reachable free coastal sites; land armies and other ships are never overwritten. Landing removes the ship and deploys its existing cargo army, spending its seasonal movement. Thereafter ordinary campaign AI handles defence, conquest, raids, merging, tactical encounters and automatic AI battles.

The faction activates with a 100-grain initial national reserve, granted only once; later naval waves do not replenish its treasury. Embarked rosters begin ordinary troop upkeep and supply/terrain wear after disembarkation. Without a city, recruitment is skipped before model inference. The strategy prompt asks the Sea Peoples to prioritize conquering their first capital. The first completed conquest records their founding capital permanently. Captured regions provide the usual occupied half-production, harvested annually, and protect occupying troops from terrain wear without providing recovery. After conquest the normal economic recruitment gate applies, and armies deploy in the capital region using Sea Peoples profiles. Further invasion waves continue regardless of territorial gains or losses.
