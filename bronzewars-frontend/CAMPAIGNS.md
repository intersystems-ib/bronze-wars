# Campaigns

The main menu's Campaigns entry opens the Egyptian campaign using `src/assets/map/egypt.png`.

## Initial rules

- A new campaign starts in 1300 BC, during Inundation, with 100 grain and no recruited armies.
- Each year has three turns: Inundation, Emergence and Harvest. After Harvest, the next year starts (1299 BC, 1298 BC, etc.). Season names follow the [Egyptian calendar](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendario_egipcio).
- Recruitment reuses the battle army builder, national profiles and grain costs returned by the existing unit catalogue. The campaign's remaining reserve is the recruitment budget; there is no separate 100-grain limit per army.
- Each army stores its composition and nation and appears with that nation's light infantry sprite. Armies muster on the first available land hex near the Nile delta.
- Movement is the unit-count-weighted mean of the composition's speeds. Only complete hexes can be crossed (a mean of 1.6 allows one hex per turn). Entering desert or mountains exhausts the remaining allowance. An army starting on either difficult terrain can move to just one neighboring hex, exhausting the turn even when leaving it. Paths cannot cross an intermediate difficult hex. Movement cannot be banked between seasons.
- Reachable destinations use breadth-first pathfinding and account for occupied hexes, difficult terrain and approximate coastlines. Rivers are traversable; sea hexes are not. Terrain regions are authored approximations of the supplied illustration in `src/config/campaignTerrain.js`: Nile valley/delta, desert, mountains and water. Each terrain type includes its movement restriction and attrition rate. Naval movement and campaign battles remain outside this phase.
- At the end of each season, deployed troops consume 10% of their recruitment grain cost (quantity × grainCost × 0.1). Fractional grain is retained; only casualty counts round up. Consumption is based on the composition before that season's losses.
- Grain is shared by all armies. Situation is evaluated at the beginning of each season's settlement: empty reserves mean famine (5% supply casualties); positive reserves with production greater than upkeep mean abundance (recover 5% of missing troops); otherwise the situation is scarcity (no supply casualties or recovery, including equal production/consumption).
- Armies ending a turn in desert or mountains suffer 3% terrain casualties, even if they did not move. Terrain losses apply first, followed by supply losses on survivors when applicable. Each percentage rounds up once per army; losses are allocated proportionally among troop types with largest remainders, avoiding extra rounding per troop type. Empty troop types and armies are removed. New compositions determine the following turn's movement and upkeep.
- A saved turn report shows grain produced/required/consumed, the applied situation, terrain and supply casualties, recovered troops and destroyed armies. Terrain and attrition are also shown when inspecting a map hex.
- Advancing the season adds production from all productive land hexes belonging to Egypt, subtracts upkeep and clamps reserves to zero. The starting reserve determines famine even if new production covers the bill that season. Neutral, Assyrian, Nubian and unowned sea hexes do not contribute to the Egyptian reserve. Movement resets after settlement.
- Recovery rounds up once per army based on 5% of its missing troops and distributes recovered troops proportionally among the missing types. Recruitment stores the original roster so each type is capped at its original quantity; fully depleted types can recover while the army survives. Armies completely destroyed by terrain or hunger do not reappear. Legacy saves without a recorded roster use their composition when first settling under these rules as the recovery baseline; their other state is preserved.
- Terrain attrition remains independent of supply situation: it applies before supply casualties or recovery. Scarcity prevents supply penalties, not terrain casualties.
- Hexes with access to water (rivers, valleys, coasts and sea) have production 1; dry desert and mountains have production 0. Coast is traversable land with zero terrain attrition. Coastline classification samples the centre, corners and edge midpoints: a mixed land/sea hex is Coast even if its centre is in the water. Land adjacent to open sea also has coastal access. Open sea remains impassable to armies.
- Clicking a non-movement destination switches the left sidebar from armies to terrain details and clears army selection. Clicking an army or using Back to your armies restores the army view; valid movement clicks retain it. Terrain details show territory, river where applicable, attrition, production and movement. Grain uses Unicode U+131E3.
- Egypt and Nubia follow the Nile valley/delta plus two neighboring land hexes on either side, using hex-grid distance. Egypt includes visible row 34; Nubia starts at row 35. Remote deserts remain neutral. Assyria covers the Jordan, Euphrates and Levant coast; Sinai is explicitly neutral. Borders follow hex edges: Egyptian yellow, Assyrian blue and Nubian purple. Shared boundaries render both nations' colors on their own side with a 4px stroke. Borders are currently visual and do not restrict army movement.

## Persistence and verification

Campaign progress is stored in the current browser's local storage under `bronzewars.campaign.egypt.v1`, independently of saved tactical battles in IRIS. Reloading or switching menu entries preserves the campaign. This first phase does not synchronize campaigns between browsers or devices.

The campaign rules live in `src/config/campaignRules.js`. Run `npm test` for recruitment, shared reserves, movement, blocked hexes, seasons and persistence checks. Run `npm run build` to validate the production frontend.
